# Intent: simplified-technical-caveman

We take the best parts of Simplified Technical English, and we force caveman
levels of tokens upon it.

Simplified Technical English (STE) earns its keep through structure and
discipline: warnings before the steps they guard, description kept apart from
procedure, one term for one meaning, one action per step, short active
sentences. Those parts stay. Its heavier machinery — the full controlled
dictionary, rigid document-module formats, and wording that grows the text to
satisfy a rule — is not the goal and is dropped when it costs tokens without
buying clarity.

Caveman supplies the density. Every prose line is cut to the fewest tokens that
keep the meaning: no filler, no hedging, no padding, no invented abbreviations.
Negations, numbers, commands, paths, and exact error strings survive untouched.

The two pull against each other only at the edges. When compression would make a
sentence ambiguous, clarity wins. When an STE rule would add words with no gain
in understanding, compression wins. The result is a document a reader can scan,
follow, and trust, at a fraction of the usual length.

It restructures one named document and invents nothing: missing facts are marked
`TBD`, never guessed. It does not turn on session-wide caveman mode.
